About this app

About DAVE Notes

A free trainer for one skill: taking notes while you listen to a real talk.

Who it is for

Students in English for Academic Purposes (EAP) and pathway courses, at about upper-intermediate level (CEFR B2). In a lecture you have to listen, decide what matters and write, all at the same time. That is hard. This app lets you practise it in short, focused sessions.

One session takes 10 to 15 minutes: get ready, learn one skill, listen and take notes, check your own notes, use them to answer questions, review, and set a goal.

How the activities were made

The activities (key words, key ideas, questions and model notes) were drafted with AI and checked by the teacher. They are written in our own words and based on the real talks.

The app does not use AI while you are using it.

Where the talks come from

  • TED talks play in TED’s own official player. The app does not copy or change them. Each talk shows the speaker, the title and a link to TED.com. Transcripts stay on TED.com. This app is not made or endorsed by TED.
  • News listenings are on Breaking News English by Sean Banville. The app links to the lesson pages and does not copy their audio or text. This app is not made or endorsed by Breaking News English.

Your data stays on this device

  • The app stores your notes and photos only on this device, in this browser.
  • Your notes, photos and recordings are never uploaded. Nobody else can see them, including the teacher.
  • There is no sign-in, no account and no tracking or analytics in this app.
  • To delete everything, go to My progress and press Clear my data.
  • The page loads fonts from Google Fonts. TED’s player is loaded from TED, and TED’s own privacy policy applies to it. No notes or personal information are sent by this app.

Why you check your own notes

Self-checking is part of the skill being trained. Good note-takers check their own notes and know what they missed. So in this app, you compare your notes with a list of key ideas that the teacher has checked.

The app does not mark your notes and it cannot read handwriting. Photos of paper notes are only there so you can look at them while you check. The numbers you see come from your own answers.

The honour rule

For TED talks, keep subtitles off. For all talks, listen once without going back, like in a real lecture. A second listen is fine, and the app records it. Nobody checks the honour rule: it is up to you, and it makes your practice real.

What the app is based on

  • Learning one small skill at a time works better than just “taking notes”.
  • Good notes have the main ideas, the important details and a clear structure. The number of words does not matter, so the app never counts them.
  • The layout of your notes matters less than what is in them. Use any layout.
  • You can use any language, symbols, arrows and drawings.
  • Notes help twice: while you write them, and when you use them later. So every session asks you to use your notes.
  • Guessing how well you did, then checking, helps you learn to judge your own learning.
